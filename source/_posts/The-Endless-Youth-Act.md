## A Personal Act for the Preservation of a Youthful Body and a Youthful Spirit

### Preamble

The title and legislative form of this piece are inspired by the [Endless Frontier Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/116th-congress/senate-bill/3832), which sought to mobilize long-term investment in scientific and technological vitality; this personal Act turns the same ambition inward.

A month ago, in [*On the Threshold of Thirty*](/2026/07/04/On-the-Threshold-of-Thirty/), we set down several strategies for meeting the uncertainty ahead: stay attentive to the land, draw strength from our past, use our tools boldly, recover our hobbies, and maintain our friendships deliberately.

**Strategies, however, remain abstract until they acquire habits.** Therefore, to mobilize a lifelong investment in physical vigor and mental vitality; to preserve enthusiasm, curiosity, and the capacity for renewal; to resist the stagnation of body, mind, and circumstance; and for other purposes, we hereby enact the following.

**Youth, for the purposes of this Act, shall not mean the denial of age.** It shall mean the continued possession of energy, curiosity, openness, courage, and appetite for life. The object is not to remain unchanged. **The object is to remain capable of change.**

## Title I — Physical Vigor

### **Section 1. Nourishment**

We shall eat food that nourishes rather than merely fills us. We shall favor nutrient-dense foods and continue learning which foods are native to the land, appropriate to the place, and in season. Eating shall be treated as one way of maintaining a relationship with the ground beneath our feet.

Vitamin and mineral supplements may be used where diet, circumstance, or medical advice makes them useful. They shall supplement food rather than excuse neglect of it.

Occasional indulgence is expressly permitted for the preservation of morale. **A sustainable discipline is preferable to a brittle purity.**

### **Section 2. Movement**

**We shall move every day.** On strong days, movement may mean strenuous exercise. On tired days, it may mean walking, stretching, or active recovery. Illness and injury shall be answered with rest and care, not self-reproach.

Exercise shall not be regarded merely as a chore performed against the body. It shall be **an expression of what the body can do, a rehearsal for continued independence, and a celebration of physical strength.**

### **Section 3. Bearing**

We shall dress with care for the occasion, not for vanity alone, but as **an act of readiness and self-respect.** We shall practice healthy posture and carry ourselves as someone still fully participating in the world.

### **Section 4. Rest**

We shall protect seven to eight hours of sleep each night whenever circumstances permit. **Rest is not time stolen from life.** It is the maintenance that makes life available to us.

## Title II — Mental Vitality

### **Section 5. Daily Substance**

**Every day should contain something that adds substance to life.** We shall reserve time and energy for reading widely, expanding our horizons, and acquiring skills whose value may not yet be visible.

We shall not permit work, feeds, errands, and passive entertainment to consume the whole field of attention.

### **Section 6. Hobbies and Useful Uselessness**

We shall deepen the hobbies we already possess and actively cultivate new ones, **even—and especially—when they are not immediately useful.**

A hobby need not become a side business, a public identity, or a source of measurable progress. Photography, music, writing, coding, travel, and interests not yet discovered may be pursued simply because they make time come alive.

**At least one part of life shall remain beyond the jurisdiction of career advancement.**

### **Section 7. Contact with Youthful Institutions**

We shall actively discover and participate in lectures, performances, clubs, workshops, and other activities held by the university and by organizations in which curiosity remains socially acceptable.

We shall not disqualify ourselves from participation merely because we are no longer the youngest person in the room. Nor shall we mistake proximity to the young for youth itself: **the purpose is to keep learning, attempting, and being surprised.**

## Title III — The Social Safety Net

### **Section 8. No Single Point of Failure**

Having chosen, at least for now, not to organize our life around finding a romantic partner and settling down, we shall not pretend that splendid isolation alone can meet every human need. We shall instead maintain **a distributed, fault-tolerant network of affection, conversation, assistance, and companionship.**

**No one person shall be expected to carry the entire weight of our loneliness.** A delayed reply shall not be interpreted as rejection, and silence from one node shall not be mistaken for the collapse of the network.

**We shall strive not only to possess a support network, but to be a dependable part of other people’s networks.** We shall listen, remember, celebrate, offer help, and make room for the needs of others without always waiting to be asked.

### **Section 9. Family and Friends**

We shall speak with family often. **We shall take the initiative with friends, old and new, rather than waiting to be remembered first.** Messages, calls, meals, walks, and visits shall be scheduled when spontaneity proves unreliable.

Instant-messaging applications shall be treated more like correspondence than emergency dispatch. We may reach out to several people without demanding an immediate answer from any one of them.

We shall also enter places where new friendships can begin. Existing relationships require maintenance; a living social world also requires renewal.

### **Section 10. Public Correspondence**

We may post honest dispatches from our life, work, travels, and interests on social media and on our own website. The purpose shall be **to make ourselves findable to like-minded people and to contribute something of substance**—not to submit our worth to an audience or an algorithm.

## Title IV — Sovereignty over Time

### **Section 11. Work and the Rest of Life**

**Work shall remain part of life and shall not annex the whole of it.** We shall take time off deliberately, before exhaustion makes the decision on our behalf.

We shall go on trips from time to time. A trip need not be distant or impressive. Its purpose is to interrupt habituation, restore attention, and remind us that the current arrangement of life is not the only possible one.

### **Section 12. Alternative Arrangements**

We shall continue investigating unconventional ways of living and working, including but not limited to financial independence, early retirement, entrepreneurship, independent creation, and other arrangements not organized around permanent dependence on a single employer.

Investigation shall not require immediate commitment. **The first obligation is to keep alternatives imaginable;** the second is to prepare patiently for those that prove worthwhile.

## Title V — Administration of the Act

### **Section 13. Minimum Practice**

The Act shall be administered through the following recurring practices:

- **Daily:** move the body, eat with some care, and add at least one thing of substance to the mind.
- **Weekly:** make deliberate contact with family or friends and spend protected time on a hobby or new interest.
- **Monthly:** attend, visit, or attempt something outside the ordinary circuit of work and home.
- **Periodically:** take time off, travel, review our way of living, and consider what has become stagnant.

**These are floors, not quotas, and guides, not instruments of punishment.**

### **Section 14. Exceptions and Resumption**

Illness, injury, grief, deadlines, travel, financial constraint, and ordinary human inconsistency may temporarily interrupt any provision of this Act.

**No lapse shall invalidate the Act.** No missed day shall be converted into a verdict on character. **Compliance resumes with the next available choice.**

### **Section 15. Review and Amendment**

This Act shall be reviewed at least once each year. Provisions that have become hollow, harmful, or obsolete may be amended. New provisions may be added as experience requires.

**The spirit of the Act shall take precedence over its letter.** Its purpose is to keep life vigorous and open, not to turn life into an inspection regime.

### **Section 16. Effective Date**

**This Act takes effect immediately and shall remain in force for as long as we remain capable of renewal.**
