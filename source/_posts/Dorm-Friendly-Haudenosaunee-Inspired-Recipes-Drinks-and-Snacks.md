This post is motivated by my experience as a Cornell student: I wanted practical, dorm-friendly ways to cook meals inspired by Haudenosaunee agricultural traditions. The goal is to make simple dishes with a microwave, mini-fridge, electric kettle, or hot plate while approaching these traditions with respect.

## Traditional Recipes

- **Three Sisters Stew:** A comforting, nutrient-dense stew made from white or flint corn, beans (such as kidney or white beans), and winter squash (like butternut or pumpkin), traditionally simmered with broth, wild herbs, or game meat like venison.
- **Haudenosaunee Corn Soup:** A staple of gatherings and ceremonies, made using white flint corn treated with wood ashes or pickling lime (nixtamalization) to remove the hulls, then cooked slowly with pork or beef chunks and kidney beans.

## Dorm-Friendly Adaptations

Adapting these traditional recipes for a Cornell dorm room - typically equipped with little more than a microwave, a mini-fridge, and maybe an electric kettle or a small hot plate - is entirely doable if you lean heavily on canned and pre-cooked ingredients.

### Dorm-Friendly Three Sisters Stew

**Ingredients**

- 1 can (15 oz) hominy, drained
- 1 can (15 oz) kidney beans, rinsed and drained
- Canned pumpkin/squash
- 2 cups broth
- Onion powder
- Garlic powder
- 1 tablespoon oil
- Salt and pepper

**Method**

1. **Build the Base:** In a large microwave-safe bowl or a pot on a hot plate, combine the broth, oil, onion/garlic, and cooked squash.
2. **Combine:** Stir in the drained hominy and beans.
3. **Heat Through:** Microwave on high for 4 to 5 minutes (or simmer on your hot plate) until piping hot and the flavors have blended. Season with salt and pepper.

### Dorm-Friendly Haudenosaunee Corn Soup

**Ingredients**

- 2 cans (15 oz each) hominy, drained
- 1 can (15 oz) kidney beans, rinsed and drained
- 2 cups broth
- Fully cooked diced ham, pre-cooked rotisserie chicken breast, or leftover bacon bits to mimic the traditional pork/meat flavor
- Salt, pepper, and a pinch of sage or garlic powder

**Method**

1. **Combine:** In a large microwave-safe bowl or pot, mix the drained hominy, kidney beans, and your pre-cooked meat of choice.
2. **Add Broth:** Pour in the broth and stir well.
3. **Simmer:** Microwave for 4 to 6 minutes (or heat on your hot plate until bubbling). Let it sit for a minute so the hominy absorbs the broth.
4. **Finish:** Season with salt, pepper, and herbs, then enjoy right out of the bowl.

## Drinks and Snacks

### Traditional Strawberry Drink (*Ohanda Dehkahwhi* style)

Strawberries hold a deeply sacred place in Haudenosaunee culture as the first berry of the season, representing thanksgiving and renewal. For a quick dorm version, mash fresh or thawed frozen strawberries with a touch of maple syrup or honey, then mix with cold water in a pitcher.

#### Dorm-Friendly Preparation

**Ingredients**

- 1 cup of fresh strawberries, or frozen strawberries thawed in your mini-fridge
- 1 to 2 tablespoons of maple syrup
- 3 to 4 cups of cold water

**Preparation**

Place the berries in a bowl or mason jar and use a fork or spoon to mash them thoroughly until they break down into a pulpy puree and release their juices. Stir in the maple syrup, then pour in the cold water. Let it steep in your fridge for 15 to 30 minutes so the water takes on a bright pink color and natural berry flavor. You can strain out the pulp for a smooth drink, or leave it in for extra fiber.

### Toasted Sunflower Seeds or Pepitas

Sunflower is one of the historic companion crops cultivated alongside the Three Sisters. Lightly toasting raw seeds with a pinch of salt makes a quick, protein-dense desk snack while studying.

### Staghorn Sumac Tea

Staghorn sumac tea (often called sumac-ade) is a cold-extracted drink made from the fuzzy, crimson cone-shaped seed clusters (drupes) of the native North American staghorn sumac tree.

**Ingredients**

- 3 to 4 whole, clean staghorn sumac seed clusters (bobs)
- 4 cups cold water
- Sweetener of choice (maple syrup, honey, or sugar to taste)

**Instructions**

1. **Cold Steep:** Place the whole clusters into a large pitcher or jar and pour the cold water over them.
2. **Extract:** Let the mixture steep in your refrigerator or at room temperature for 3 to 4 hours (or overnight). The water will turn a lovely pinkish-red color.
3. **Strain:** Remove the seed heads and discard them. Carefully pour the liquid through a fine-mesh sieve lined with a coffee filter or cheesecloth to catch the tiny hairs and fine sediment.
4. **Sweeten and Serve:** Stir in maple syrup or honey to taste. Serve chilled over ice.
